A Little Science Can Make It Feel Less Mysterious

Knowledge helps soothe fear by replacing the unknown with calm understanding.

⚡

Rapid Air Expansion

Lightning heats the surrounding air extremely quickly. The rapid expansion and collapse of that heated air creates the acoustic shockwave we hear as thunder.

🌟

Light vs. Sound

We see lightning before hearing thunder because light travels at roughly 300,000 km/s, while sound travels at approximately 340 meters per second.

🛡

Thunder Cannot Hurt You

Thunder is simply acoustic vibrations in the atmosphere. The sound itself cannot harm you. Staying sheltered indoors keeps you safely away from electrical hazards.

🏠

Buildings Offer Shelter

Modern buildings with plumbing, electrical wiring, and grounded structures naturally guide electrical charges harmlessly into the earth.

Make Your Space Feel Safer

Practical, actionable steps to feel grounded and secure indoors.

During a thunderstorm, stay inside a substantial building or enclosed vehicle when possible. Safe indoor structures have protective grounding paths that disperse electricity safely into the ground.

Stay away from windows, doors, balconies, and exposed outdoor areas. Draw curtains or blinds shut if seeing sudden flashes triggers anxiety; this softens visual stimuli while keeping you sheltered.

Avoid using corded electrical equipment or touching metal plumbing during severe thunderstorms, and follow local safety guidance. Cordless devices and cell phones are safe to use.

Try headphones, calming music, white noise, or a familiar comforting TV show if thunder feels overwhelming. Creating an acoustic barrier gently reduces acoustic startling.

Use a trusted official weather service or local emergency alert system for storm updates. Avoid continuous doom-scrolling; checking in once or twice is enough to stay informed and safe.

Fear is a natural, protective biological response. You don't have to force yourself to feel completely calm immediately. Focus simply on the next small step, and let your body settle in its own time.

⚠️ When the storm is nearby

  • Stay indoors in a substantial building
  • Avoid open spaces and high ground
  • Stay away from glass windows and doors
  • Follow local weather alerts and official guidance
  • If authorities issue emergency instructions, follow them

This app provides general calming and safety information. It is not a substitute for emergency services, professional mental-health care, or official weather warnings.
